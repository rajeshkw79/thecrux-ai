# The Founder Brief: Priya Nair & theboringtasks.com

## Meet Priya Nair

Priya is 39. She lives in Indiranagar, Bangalore, with her husband (engineering lead at a fintech) and an opinionated 7-year-old who has opinions about font sizes.

She is a second-time founder. Her first company, **Hired.co.in**, was a B2B hiring automation tool for mid-sized Indian companies — it parsed job descriptions, matched candidates, and auto-scheduled interviews. She bootstrapped it to 80 paying customers (₹3.2 crore ARR), took a small angel round, and sold it to **PeopleStrong** in late 2022 for ₹91 crore. She worked there as VP Product through the integration, got bored in month 8, and resigned politely.

She spent 2023 doing what she called "productive procrastination" — advising 4 startups, investing in 3, and quietly obsessing over one pattern she kept seeing everywhere.

---

## The Genesis Story

After the exit, Priya started visiting her portfolio companies one by one — sitting with their teams, watching how work actually happened.

What she saw was almost identical at every company: smart, expensive people spending 30–40% of their week on tasks that were neither strategic nor creative. They were just *repetitive*.

A PM reformatting the same status report every Friday.
A sales lead copy-pasting deal details from CRM into a pipeline email.
A finance analyst running the same month-end reconciliation macro, then spending 45 minutes cleaning the output.
An operations manager answering the same 12 supplier questions every Monday.

None of these people thought of themselves as doing "low-value" work. They thought of it as "part of the job." But when Priya added it up — across a 200-person company — it was the equivalent of 60 full-time employees doing nothing but boring tasks.

The insight that crystallised this for her came from a conversation with Rohan, a VP of Ops at a logistics firm:

> "I know AI can do amazing things. But honestly? I just want something that does the boring things reliably, without drama, and without me having to explain it every time."

That sentence became the product thesis.

**The boring tasks are not the exception. They are the steady drain. And no one had built an AI product specifically for that.**

---

## The Product: theboringtasks.com

**One line:** AI agents that learn your repetitive knowledge work and run it for you — reliably, every time, without you re-explaining.

**What it does:**

theboringtasks.com is a set of AI agents purpose-built for recurring, structured knowledge work. You teach it a task once — by doing it while it watches, or by describing it in plain language — and it handles it from then on.

**Core use cases at launch:**
1. **Weekly reports** — auto-drafts status updates, pipeline reports, and board summaries by pulling from connected tools (email, spreadsheets, Notion, Slack)
2. **Follow-up sequences** — generates contextual follow-ups for sales, ops, and client management without copy-paste
3. **Document formatting** — takes messy inputs (meeting notes, voice memos, raw data) and outputs clean formatted documents in your house style
4. **Data cleanup** — reconciles spreadsheets, normalises formats, flags anomalies, and produces ready-to-send summaries
5. **Meeting summaries** — listens to or reads transcripts, extracts decisions, action items, and open questions in your format
6. **Inbox triage** — categorises, drafts responses, and flags priority emails by context, not just keywords

**What makes it different:**
- It does *boring* reliably, not impressive occasionally. Most AI tools demo beautifully and fail on the 100th repetition.
- It learns YOUR format, YOUR voice, YOUR definitions. Not a generic output.
- It runs on a schedule — weekly, daily, Monday morning. You don't prompt it. It just runs.
- It keeps an audit log of every task it ran, every output it produced. Full accountability.

**What it is NOT:**
- Not a chatbot. Not a general-purpose assistant.
- Not a coding tool.
- Not another Notion AI or Copilot inside an existing tool.
- It is its own product, focused entirely on eliminating the boring stack.

**Priya's take on the positioning:** "Everyone is building AI that does impressive things. We're building AI that does *reliable* things. The boring things. The things your team does every week that they hate but can't stop doing. That's the market."

## Vision & Mission

**Mission:** To give every knowledge worker back the hours they spend doing work that should already be automated.

**Vision:** A world where AI handles the bottom 30% of every professional's task list — not because those tasks don't matter, but because they're too important to be done inconsistently and too repetitive to need a human every time.

## What Priya Wants from the Website

This is not a brochure site. It needs to do three things:

1. **Make the pain obvious** — Visitors should see themselves in the first 10 seconds. "That's exactly what I do every Monday."
2. **Make the product legible** — In plain language, before any signup. No "unlock the power of AI" vagueness.
3. **Convert to the waitlist** — Primary CTA at launch is "Join the waitlist." There is no free trial yet. The goal is 5,000 waitlist signups by India launch.

Priya has said: "If someone reads our homepage and thinks we're a chatbot, we've failed. If they read it and immediately think of the task they did last Tuesday that they hated — we've won."
