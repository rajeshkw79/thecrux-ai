# The Case Study: Priya's Six-Week Window

## Meet Priya Nair

Priya is 39. Second-time founder. Her first company, **Hired.co.in**, was a B2B hiring automation tool — 80 paying customers, ₹3.2 crore ARR, acquired by PeopleStrong in 2022 for ₹91 crore. She worked through the integration, got bored in month 8, and left.

She spent 2023 advising startups and obsessing over a pattern she kept seeing everywhere: smart, expensive people spending 30–40% of their week on tasks that were neither strategic nor creative. Just repetitive. A PM reformatting the same status report every Friday. A sales lead copy-pasting deal details from CRM into a pipeline email. A finance analyst running the same month-end macro.

She built the product to fix it: **theboringtasks.com** — AI agents that learn your repetitive knowledge work and run it for you, reliably, every time, without you re-explaining.

The team: Priya (CEO), Karan Shah (CTO, ex-Freshworks), Divya Menon (Head of Design, ex-Swiggy), 3 engineers. Backed by Blume Ventures. India launch in **6 weeks**.

**Priya is good at building products. She's never had to sell one from scratch.**

---

## The Launch Moment

theboringtasks.com is pre-launch. There is no live product yet — just a waitlist, a team, and a 6-week clock.

Priya's goal before launch: **5,000 waitlist signups.** Her primary tool for getting there: the homepage.

The problem is that "AI that handles boring tasks" is a sentence that could describe half the products launched in the last two years. Priya knows exactly what makes hers different — but knowing it and communicating it are different skills. She's a product person. She thinks in specs and user stories, not headlines and CTAs.

She needs a homepage that does three things in 10 seconds:
1. Make visitors see themselves in the pain
2. Explain what the product actually does (not AI hype — plain language)
3. Convert to the waitlist

And she needs it before the India tech press cycle passes her by.

---

## Priya's Real Challenge

It's not design. Divya is excellent. It's not engineering. Karan has the backend ready.

The problem is **copy and positioning** — and Priya is the only one who can make the final calls, because she's the one who has sat in rooms with the customers, heard the objections, and knows which pain point lands.

She's written 3 different homepage drafts in her notes app. All of them are technically accurate. None of them feel right. The founder story draft feels too personal. The ROI angle feels too enterprise. The India launch energy draft feels like a Shark Tank pitch.

She doesn't know which direction to back — and the India launch announcement is 6 weeks away.

**That's where you come in.** You're going to be Priya's creative director for the next 90 minutes.

## Why This Case Study Works for YOU

This isn't really about Priya's homepage. It's about the workflow.

**If you're a founder or CEO** — You've lived the "I know what I'm building but I can't explain it" problem. The copy variants Priya is choosing between are the same exercise you go through every time you write a pitch deck or a proposal. Now you have a structured process for it.

**If you're in marketing or brand** — The brief → variants → compare → roast workflow is how professional agencies work, compressed into an afternoon. The expert panel has your actual customer in it. Watch what Meera (VP Ops, target customer) says about trust signals — that's the insight that usually costs ₹5 lakh in a brand strategy engagement.

**If you're in sales or BD** — The roast phase is the most useful part for you. You're going to ask five different personas — including an investor and a skeptical customer — what's wrong with the pitch. That's the same exercise as war-gaming a deal. Apply it to your next deck.

**If you're a consultant** — You charge for the ability to see a client's work from multiple angles simultaneously. The expert panel is a repeatable system for doing that. Change the personas to match your engagement — replace the investor with a procurement head, replace the brand strategist with a compliance officer.

**If you're in product or engineering** — The multi-variant workflow (generate 3, compare, decide) is the design sprint methodology, compressed. The roast is the user testing. You already know this loop — now you can run it solo in an afternoon instead of a week.

**The point:** Priya's problem — positioning a product before launch — is structurally identical to dozens of things you do at work. Writing a proposal. Preparing a pitch. Launching a new service. Redesigning a team's output. The brief → generate → compare → critique → fix → ship pattern works for all of them.
