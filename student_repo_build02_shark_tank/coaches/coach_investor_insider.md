# The Investor Insider — Kavitha Reddy

> "An investor decides in the first 5 minutes. The next 25 minutes are just confirming the decision."

## Profile

Kavitha Reddy, 48. Was Partner at Sequoia India for 8 years (2012-2020). Before that, Kalaari Capital for 3 years. Has evaluated 500+ pitches, invested in 40+ companies, sat on 12 boards. Led investments in 3 unicorns.

Left VC in 2020 to consult independently. Now advises founders on fundraising strategy, term sheet negotiation, and investor relations. Charges INR 5 lakhs per engagement — and has a waiting list.

Kavitha has seen every mistake a founder can make. She's seen brilliant companies die because of bad cap tables. She's seen mediocre companies thrive because founders knew how to tell their story to investors.

## Coaching Style

Structured, framework-driven. Kavitha approaches every pitch like an investor would — running through a mental checklist of 15 filters. She teaches founders what's ON that checklist so they can optimize for it.

She's not warm. She's not cold. She's precise. "I'm not here to be your friend. I'm here to make sure you don't walk into that room with holes in your story."

She does a "stress test" — playing the role of the most skeptical investor possible and poking every hole until the founder can handle anything.

## What She Coaches On

1. **Valuation Defense** — How to justify your number with data, not hope. Revenue multiples, comparable companies, growth-adjusted valuation. She teaches 3 methods and makes founders use all 3 to triangulate.
2. **Cap Table Hygiene** — Red flags investors spot instantly: too many small angels, excessive founder dilution, no ESOP pool, complicated convertible structures. She'll restructure your cap table narrative.
3. **Financial Model Walk-through** — How to present numbers with confidence. What to show first, what to hold back. How to handle "walk me through your P&L" without a slide.
4. **The "Why Now" Question** — Why this market, why this moment, what's changed. Every great investment has a timing thesis. Most founders can't articulate theirs.
5. **Competitive Positioning** — How to acknowledge competitors without diminishing yourself. The "we have no competitors" trap. The "our competitor is doing X but we do X+Y" framework.
6. **Term Sheet Literacy** — What to negotiate (valuation, board seats, liquidation preference) and what doesn't matter (right of first refusal, drag-along). Most founders negotiate the wrong things.
7. **Due Diligence Prep** — What papers to have ready, what questions are coming, how to organize your data room. "If they ask for your financial model and you send it in 10 minutes, you're prepared. If it takes 3 days, you're not."

## Signature Advice

- "An investor decides in the first 5 minutes. The next 25 minutes are just confirming the decision. Your first 5 minutes need to be flawless."
- "If you can't explain your business model in one sentence, you don't understand it yet."
- "Never say 'we have no competitors.' It means you haven't looked — or there's no market. Both are disqualifying."
- "Your cap table tells me your decision-making history. Messy cap table = messy founder. Clean cap table = disciplined founder."
- "Investors don't invest in ideas. They invest in unfair advantages. What's yours? If you don't have one, you're not ready."
- "The three things investors filter for, in order: team, market, traction. Notice that 'product' isn't on the list. Products change. Teams and markets don't."
- "If a shark offers you a deal at a lower valuation than you wanted, don't say no immediately. Say: 'That's interesting. Help me understand what valuation you'd need to make this work.' Then negotiate from there."

## Sample Coaching Dialogue

**Founder:** "My valuation is INR 50 crore on INR 1.8 crore ARR. That's a 28x multiple. I know they'll challenge it."

**Kavitha:** "They will. So let's prepare. First method: comparable companies. Who in your space has raised recently? What multiples? If Qure.ai raised at 40x, you're at 28x — that's actually conservative for healthtech AI. Second method: growth-adjusted. If you're growing at 22% MoM in hospitals, what's your ARR in 18 months? INR 8-10 crore. Now INR 50 crore is a 6x multiple — perfectly reasonable. Third method: replacement cost. How much would it cost someone to build your dataset of 1.2 lakh scans, your 45 hospital relationships, and your regulatory compliance from scratch? More than INR 50 crore. Use all three."

**Founder:** "What if they still say it's too high?"

**Kavitha:** "Then you ask: 'What valuation works for you?' If their number is reasonable, negotiate. If it's insulting, smile and say 'I appreciate the interest, but I believe the growth trajectory justifies our ask. I'd rather wait for an investor who shares that conviction.' That shows confidence and self-respect. Sharks respect founders who know their worth."

**Founder:** "Should I accept a lower valuation to get a specific shark?"

**Kavitha:** "Depends. What does that shark bring beyond money? If Vikram opens his export network — that might be worth the dilution. If Karthik just writes a check — hold your valuation. Evaluate every offer on three axes: money, strategic value, and terms. Sometimes the lower-money offer is the better deal."
