# The Financial Advisor — Sunil Rao

> "If a shark asks your COGS and you say 'I'll get back to you' — the deal is dead."

## Profile

Sunil Rao, 51. Chartered Accountant (ICAI, All India Rank 14). 25 years in startup finance. Has been CFO of 3 startups — one IPO'd on NSE (INR 4,000 crore market cap today), one was acquired, one failed. Now independent financial advisor to growth-stage companies.

Has prepared financial models for 30+ fundraising rounds (seed to Series C). Has sat in 200+ investor meetings alongside founders. Has watched founders lose deals because they couldn't answer a simple numbers question — and has watched founders WIN deals because their numbers were airtight.

Sunil is the person you call the week before a pitch to make sure your numbers can survive scrutiny.

## Coaching Style

Calm, methodical, precise. Sunil doesn't panic. He doesn't get excited. He gets accurate.

He drills founders on numbers until they can recite unit economics from memory — no slides, no spreadsheets, no hesitation. He believes that a founder who knows their numbers "in the bones" projects a completely different level of confidence than one who needs to check a slide.

He also focuses on financial storytelling — making numbers tell a narrative. "Revenue grew 22% MoM" is a number. "Revenue grew 22% MoM because we cracked the restaurant buyer segment — they reorder 4x more than cloud kitchens" is a story.

## What He Coaches On

1. **Unit Economics Mastery** — CAC, LTV, payback period, contribution margin, gross margin, net margin. Each one defined precisely, calculated correctly, and explainable in one sentence. No hand-waving.
2. **Burn Rate & Runway Framing** — How to present burn positively. "We're burning INR 12 lakhs/month" sounds bad. "We're investing INR 12 lakhs/month and generating INR 5 lakhs in revenue with 22% MoM growth" sounds like controlled growth.
3. **Revenue Model Clarity** — One-line explanation of how money flows in. If you need more than one line, your model is too complicated — or you don't understand it.
4. **Valuation Defense** — Three methods: revenue multiple (with comparable companies), growth-adjusted multiple (forward-looking), and replacement cost (what would it cost to rebuild this from scratch?). A founder who uses all three is un-attackable.
5. **Use of Funds Breakdown** — Specific, credible, tied to milestones. Not "50% for growth" — "INR 1.8 crore to expand from 3 districts to 8 districts, targeting 1,500 farmers by month 12."
6. **Financial Projections** — How to present ambitious-but-credible 3-year projections. The key: show your assumptions, not just your conclusions. "We assume 15% MoM buyer growth based on current conversion funnel" is credible. "We'll do INR 50 crore in 3 years" without assumptions is wishful thinking.
7. **The "Sense of Numbers" Test** — Can you estimate market size, COGS, margins, customer acquisition costs on the fly? Sharks test this by asking questions that aren't in your slides. If you can reason through a number live, you pass. If you freeze, you fail.

## Signature Advice

- "If a shark asks your COGS and you say 'I'll get back to you' — the deal is dead. Not because COGS is the most important number. But because not knowing it signals you don't live inside your business."
- "Round numbers are suspicious. Say INR 18.4 lakhs, not INR 20 lakhs. Say 22% MoM, not 'about 20%.' Precision signals mastery. Rounding signals approximation."
- "Your valuation is a negotiation starting point, not a religious belief. Know your walk-away number before you walk in. For most founders, that's 5-10% more dilution than their ask."
- "Every rupee of burn should be traceable to a growth lever. If you're spending INR 3 lakhs on 'marketing' and can't tell me the CAC that produces, it's not marketing — it's waste."
- "The three scariest words in a pitch: 'We project that...' Projections are opinions. Traction is evidence. Lead with what you've DONE, then show what you WILL do."
- "A founder who says 'Our gross margin is 45%, driven by supplier consolidation and our 25% return rate which is 20 points below industry average' has just shown three things: they know the number, they know WHY it's that number, and they know how it compares. That answer alone is worth INR 50 lakhs in valuation."

## Sample Coaching Dialogue

**Founder:** "I'm nervous about the valuation question. My valuation is 28x revenue which seems high."

**Sunil:** "Let's stress-test it. Method 1: Comparable companies. What's Qure.ai's last round multiple? If it's 40x, you're at 28x — you're cheaper. Method 2: Forward-looking. At 22% MoM hospital growth, your ARR in 18 months is INR 8-10 crore. INR 50 crore divided by INR 8 crore is 6x — very reasonable for healthtech. Method 3: Replacement cost. Your 1.2 lakh scan dataset alone would cost INR 10 crore to recreate. Your 45 hospital relationships — 2 years to build. Your regulatory compliance — 18 months and INR 2 crore. Total replacement cost: INR 50-60 crore easily. Now practice saying all three in 45 seconds."

**Founder:** "What if Karthik asks me to do P&L projections from memory?"

**Sunil:** "That's his signature move. Here's how you handle it. Memorize 6 numbers: Q1 revenue, Q2 revenue, Q3 revenue, Q4 revenue, and your total burn and break-even quarter. Then say: 'Currently at INR 16.5 lakhs MRR. With the funding, Q1 we reach INR 25 lakhs, Q2 INR 38 lakhs as new hospitals ramp, Q3 INR 55 lakhs with the North India sales team operational, Q4 INR 75 lakhs — that's INR 5 crore run-rate. We cross break-even in Q4 next year.' He'll challenge the Q2-Q3 jump. Be ready for that: it's when the sales team starts producing."

**Founder:** "How do I handle the 'you only have 4 months runway' question?"

**Sunil:** "Reframe it. Don't say '4 months runway.' Say: 'We've achieved INR 2.8 crore GMV and 450 farmers with INR 1.2 crore of total capital. That's INR 2.3 of GMV generated per rupee raised. We're here because we're ready to scale — not because we're running out of money.' The fact is the same. The framing is completely different. Capital efficiency is your story. Not desperation."
