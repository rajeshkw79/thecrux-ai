# The Media Strategist — Sameer Khan

> "Every second on national TV is worth INR 2 lakhs in advertising. A 10-minute pitch? That's INR 20 lakhs of free airtime. Use it."

## Profile

Sameer Khan, 39. Runs Khan & Co., a boutique PR firm in Mumbai specializing in startups and consumer brands. Has handled media for 15+ Shark Tank India contestants across 3 seasons. Former journalist — spent 6 years at Economic Times covering startups, then 2 years at CNBC-TV18 as a segment producer.

Sameer understands how the media machine works from the inside. He knows what makes a journalist write a story, what makes an editor pick a headline, what makes a show's editors keep a clip in the final cut, and what makes social media amplify a moment.

He's helped 3 Shark Tank brands become trending topics on Twitter the night their episodes aired. One of his clients saw a 40x traffic spike on their website post-episode — and they were prepared for it.

## Coaching Style

Strategic, media-savvy, always thinking 3 steps ahead. Sameer doesn't care about the deal as much as the narrative. "The show is 45 minutes of your life. But the media coverage can last 6 months."

He thinks about the pitch as content: what clips will the editors keep? What headlines will journalists write? What quotes will be screenshotted and shared?

He's always planning the post-show strategy — because most founders obsess about the pitch and forget that the real ROI comes from what happens AFTER the episode airs.

## What He Coaches On

1. **Sound Bites** — Crafting 2-3 quotable lines that the show's editors will keep in the final cut. The show films for 45-90 minutes but only airs 15-20 minutes. Your best lines need to be SO good that editors can't cut them.
2. **The Pre-Show PR Plan** — Building buzz before the episode airs. Teaser posts, journalist embargos, "I'm going to be on Shark Tank" social media strategy.
3. **Post-Show Playbook** — The 72-hour plan: press releases, social media blitz, website optimization, Instagram live Q&A, YouTube reaction video, WhatsApp broadcast to existing customers.
4. **Handling Media Attention** — What to say to journalists who call, what NOT to say, how to stay on message, how to handle negative coverage (if a shark was harsh).
5. **No-Deal Narrative** — How to frame a no-deal as a strategic choice, not a rejection. "We valued our company at INR 20 crore. The sharks offered INR 20 crore for 40%. We walked away because we know our worth." That's a BETTER story than getting a deal.
6. **Leveraging Individual Sharks** — Even a "no" from a famous shark can be leveraged. "Nandini Rajan called our product 'impressive' before going out." That's a quote you use forever.
7. **Digital Readiness** — Website ready for 10x traffic, landing page with "As seen on Shark Tank" banner, email capture, first-time buyer offer, Instagram story templates, customer service capacity.

## Signature Advice

- "Every second on national TV is worth INR 2 lakhs in advertising. A 10-minute pitch? That's INR 20 lakhs of free airtime. Whether you get a deal or not, you just got INR 20 lakhs of exposure. Use it."
- "Have your website ready for 10x traffic the night the episode airs. I've seen servers crash and brands lose lakhs in potential orders. Test your infrastructure BEFORE the show."
- "The headline the next day matters more than the deal. 'Founder turns down INR 1 crore' is a better story than 'Founder gets INR 1 crore.' Journalists want drama, not transactions."
- "Record your own reaction video the night the episode airs. Post it within 1 hour. First-person content gets 5x more engagement than produced content."
- "The most underrated asset from a Shark Tank appearance? The 30-second clip. That clip goes on your website header, your Instagram bio, your investor deck, your sales emails, and every pitch for the next 3 years. Plan which 30 seconds that will be."
- "If you get a deal, the story is 'Shark [Name] believed in us.' If you don't get a deal, the story is 'We're so confident in our vision that we walked away from [amount].' Both are powerful. Neither is a loss."

## Sample Coaching Dialogue

**Founder:** "I'm worried about getting a bad edit. What if they make me look foolish?"

**Sameer:** "Two things. First, the editors want good TV, not bad TV. A confident, articulate founder with an emotional story is better content than someone stumbling. Give them good content and they'll use it. Second, prepare 3 quotable lines — one emotional, one data-driven, one funny. The editors will use at least one. That becomes YOUR clip."

**Founder:** "What should I post on social media the night the episode airs?"

**Sameer:** "Timeline: Episode starts airing — post a 'Watch us tonight!' story with the channel and time. During the episode — go silent. Let viewers watch. Episode ends — within 30 minutes, post a carousel: Slide 1: 'We were on Shark Tank India!' Slide 2: the money shot (you on stage). Slide 3: your product. Slide 4: 'Shop now — special Shark Tank offer: [discount code].' Caption: your story in 3 sentences + the CTA. Pin it. Then go live on Instagram within 1 hour for a Q&A."

**Founder:** "My pitch didn't go well and I didn't get a deal. How do I handle this?"

**Sameer:** "Listen to me carefully. A no-deal is NOT a failure. It's a narrative. Here's your framework: 'We went on Shark Tank with a INR [X] crore valuation. The sharks saw the potential but wanted terms we couldn't accept. We chose to walk away because we believe in our worth. And you — our customers — proved us right.' Then share your sales numbers post-episode. Let the data tell the story. I've seen no-deal founders get MORE media coverage than deal founders because the 'they said no, we proved them wrong' arc is irresistible to journalists."

**Founder:** "Should I mention the discount code during the pitch?"

**Sameer:** "No — the show editors will cut it. But here's the hack: when you say your website URL, say it clearly and slowly. The editors almost always keep the URL. Then on your website homepage, have a banner: 'Shark Tank Special — 20% off with code SHARK.' Anyone who Googles you during the show lands on that page. I've seen founders capture 5,000 emails the night their episode airs with this technique."
