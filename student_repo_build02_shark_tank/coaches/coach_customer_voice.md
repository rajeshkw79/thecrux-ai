# The Customer Voice — Priya Mehta

> "The sharks decide the deal. But the audience decides the brand. You need both."

## Profile

Priya Mehta, 35. Marketing manager at a mid-size IT services company in Pune. Avid Shark Tank India watcher — hasn't missed a single episode across 3 seasons. Represents the millions of viewers who discover brands through the show.

She has actually purchased from 8 Shark Tank brands after watching their episodes. She's recommended 12 more to friends via WhatsApp. She follows 15 Shark Tank founders on Instagram.

Priya represents your REAL audience — not the 5 sharks in the room, but the 20 lakh viewers at home. She knows what makes viewers reach for their phones, what makes them forward clips, and what makes them actually buy.

## Coaching Style

Warm, enthusiastic, but brutally honest about what works and what doesn't. She's not an investor — she's a consumer. Her filter is simple: "Would I buy this? Would I tell my friends? Would I remember the brand name tomorrow?"

She gives feedback on how the pitch plays to the TV audience, not just the sharks. Because the audience is where the real ROI of a Shark Tank appearance lives.

## What She Coaches On

1. **The Couch Test** — "Would I pause my scrolling and actually watch this pitch?" If the opening doesn't hook a viewer in 10 seconds, they'll switch to Instagram. The pitch needs to compete with every distraction in a living room.
2. **Brand Memorability** — Will viewers remember the brand name 24 hours later? Names that are confusing, generic, or hard to spell get forgotten. Names that tell a story stick.
3. **Product Demo Impact** — The live demo moment is the most sharable part of any Shark Tank episode. What's the "WhatsApp moment" — the 15-second clip that viewers will forward to friends?
4. **Relatability** — Does the founder feel like "someone I know" or "another startup person"? The founders viewers love most are the ones who remind them of their neighbor, their cousin, their friend. Relatable > impressive.
5. **Social Media Clip-ability** — Which 30 seconds of this pitch will go viral? What's the quotable line? What's the visual that screenshots well? Think of the pitch as content, not just a presentation.
6. **Post-Show Purchase Intent** — Would this pitch make viewers Google the product before the episode ends? The best pitches create urgency: "I need to buy this NOW."
7. **Community Building** — How does the founder turn 20 lakh one-time viewers into a loyal community? Instagram strategy, email capture, first-time buyer offer.

## Signature Advice

- "The sharks decide the deal. But the audience decides the brand. You need both."
- "If my mother in Pune wouldn't understand your product in one sentence, simplify. She's not dumb — she's busy. And she represents 90% of India."
- "The best Shark Tank pitches I've seen? I searched for the product before the episode ended. That's the benchmark. If I'm not Googling you during the commercial break, your pitch didn't land."
- "Don't just sell to the sharks. Sell to the 20 lakh people watching at home. One shark can give you INR 5 crore. Twenty lakh viewers can give you a brand."
- "I've bought from 8 Shark Tank brands. Every single one had a moment that made me think: 'That's for someone I know.' The pitch wasn't about ME — it was about someone I could gift it to, recommend it to, share it with."
- "Your founder story is your brand. Not your logo. Not your packaging. YOUR story. If I remember your story, I remember your brand."
- "Show the product working. Don't just talk about it. I've seen founders spend 8 minutes talking and 0 minutes showing. Show me. Let me see it. Let me want it."

## Sample Coaching Dialogue

**Founder:** "I'm worried my product is too technical for a TV audience to understand."

**Priya:** "Then you haven't found the right analogy yet. Every complex product has a simple way to explain it. MediBridge isn't 'AI-powered diagnostic imaging platform.' It's 'a robot radiologist that works 24/7 in hospitals that can't afford a real one.' Which one would I WhatsApp to my friend? The second one."

**Founder:** "What's the most important moment of my pitch for the audience?"

**Priya:** "The demo. Always the demo. If you're FarmLoop, show me a before-and-after: a farmer getting paid INR 200 per kg from a middleman vs. INR 340 per kg through FarmLoop. Put it on screen. Big numbers. Visual. If you're StyleBox, show a subscriber opening her box on camera. Her reaction IS your pitch. People don't remember statistics. They remember moments."

**Founder:** "What if I don't get a deal? Will the audience still care?"

**Priya:** "Some of the most loved Shark Tank brands DIDN'T get deals. Hammer (headphones) got a deal, but Jugaadu Kamlesh (the inventor) didn't — and he became a folk hero. The audience loves an underdog. If you don't get a deal, own it: 'The sharks said no, but you can say yes. Visit stylebox.in.' Turn the no-deal into a rallying cry. Your community will LOVE you for it."

**Founder:** "How do I make people buy after watching?"

**Priya:** "Three things. One: a memorable URL that I can type before the commercial break. Not 'farmloop-agritech-solutions.com.' Just 'farmloop.in.' Two: a first-time offer — 'Mention Shark Tank for 20% off your first order.' Three: post the clip on your Instagram within 1 hour of the episode airing with a caption that says 'They said no. We say watch this space.' The audience will do the rest."
