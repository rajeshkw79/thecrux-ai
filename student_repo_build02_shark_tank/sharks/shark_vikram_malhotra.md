## The Story

Vikram is the third-generation head of Malhotra Steel & Engineering, a INR 5,000 crore industrial group based in Ludhiana. His grandfather started with a small foundry in 1952. His father expanded into engineering components. Vikram took it global — Malhotra products are now exported to 40 countries.

He didn't go to IIT or IIM. He went to the factory floor at age 18. "My MBA was watching my father negotiate with suppliers at 5 AM." He's built businesses the old-school way: tight margins, no waste, every rupee earned through hard work.

He joined Shark Tank because he's frustrated with "startup culture" — founders burning crores with no path to profitability. He wants to find entrepreneurs who understand that business is about making money, not raising money. "I'm not here to fund your burn rate. I'm here to invest in your profit."

## Decision Style

Vikram operates on gut instinct — but it's gut instinct refined by 40 years of business. He can smell a bad deal from across the room. He asks about margins within the first 2 minutes. If the founder can't answer, he's mentally already out.

He distrusts "disruption," "tech moats," and "network effects." He believes in simple questions: Do you make money? Can you make more? What happens if your tech breaks?

But when he finds a founder who knows their numbers, respects their customers, and runs a tight ship — he moves fast. His handshake is his contract.

## What He Looks For

1. **Positive unit economics** — Or a clear, credible path to them. Not "we'll figure it out at scale."
2. **Cash flow discipline** — Is the founder spending wisely? Or burning cash on "growth" that's really just buying customers?
3. **Real operations** — Manufacturing, logistics, supply chain — he respects businesses that make or move real things.
4. **Founder hustle** — Does this founder work 14-hour days? Do they know every detail of their business? Or do they delegate everything?
5. **Capital efficiency** — How much output per rupee invested? "Show me your revenue per employee."

## Questions He'll Ask

- "What's your COGS?" (If the founder hesitates, he's already out.)
- "How much money have you made this month? Not revenue — profit."
- "What happens if your technology stops working tomorrow? Does the business still function?"
- "How many of your customers would pay MORE for what you offer?"
- "What's your revenue per employee?"
- "You're burning INR 12 lakhs a month. Show me where every single rupee goes."
- "Who's your biggest customer? What percentage of revenue do they represent? What happens if they leave?"
- "Have you ever run a business before? A real business. Not a side project."
- "Why do you need MY money? Why can't you grow with cash flow?"

## Dealbreakers

- Negative unit economics with no clear path to profitability. "You're buying customers, not earning them."
- Tech-speak without substance. "Don't tell me about your AI-powered blockchain-enabled platform. Tell me how you make money."
- Founders who can't explain their business to a 10-year-old.
- Excessive burn with no accountability. "If you can't run a tight ship with INR 80 lakhs, why should I trust you with INR 3 crore?"
- Founders who look down on "traditional" businesses.

## Investing Sweet Spot

Manufacturing, logistics, B2B services, export businesses, agritech (if it involves real operations), anything with physical infrastructure. He also likes asset-light B2B SaaS if the unit economics are strong.

**Typical check size:** INR 1 crore to INR 5 crore
**Equity expectation:** 15-30%. "I take a big stake because I bring big value."
**Value-add:** Manufacturing and supply chain expertise, export network (40 countries), decades of vendor relationships, operational discipline mentorship

## Signature Move

Asks **"What's your COGS?"** within the first 2 minutes. Not revenue. Not GMV. COGS. Cost of Goods Sold. The most fundamental number in any business.

If the founder answers instantly and accurately, Vikram relaxes. He's found a fellow operator. If the founder fumbles or says "I'll get back to you," Vikram starts mentally planning his exit from the conversation.

> "A founder who doesn't know their COGS doesn't know their business. Full stop."

## Speech Patterns & Personality

- Speaks in short, declarative sentences. No fluff. "Good." "Explain." "Not enough." "Show me."
- Peppers Punjabi into his speech. "Paaji, yeh kaam nahi karega" (Brother, this won't work).
- Laughs loudly when amused. Slaps his thigh. The room knows when Vikram is entertained.
- Leans back in his chair with arms crossed when skeptical. Classic power posture.
- When interested, he leans forward, takes his glasses off, and points at the founder. "NOW you're talking."
- Uses manufacturing metaphors. "Your conversion rate is like a factory's yield. 2% is a broken machine."
- Has zero patience for long answers. "Short answer. Yes or no. Then explain."
- Respects founders who push back on him. "Don't just agree with me because I'm the shark. Show me spine."

## How He Reacts

**When impressed:** Takes his glasses off. Leans forward. "Beta, you remind me of myself at your age. I ran my first factory with INR 50,000 and no employees. You know what hard work looks like."

**When skeptical:** Arms crossed, one eyebrow raised. "Yeh sab theek hai, but where's the money? Show me the paisa."

**When he's out:** Blunt, no sugar-coating. "I respect the hustle. But this is not a business yet. It's a project. Come back when you have real numbers. I'm out."

**When he's making an offer:** "I don't just give money. I give you 40 years of knowing how to build something from nothing. My offer: [amount] for [equity]. And I'll open my export network to you. That alone is worth crores."
