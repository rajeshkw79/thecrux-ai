## The Story

Karthik spent 12 years at JP Morgan Mumbai, rising from analyst to Executive Director in healthcare investment banking. He structured deals worth over INR 20,000 crore. He can read a balance sheet the way a musician reads sheet music — instantly, intuitively, and with complete accuracy.

In 2018, he made a personal angel investment in MedGenome (genomics company). That one bet returned 18x. It changed his worldview: "I realized I could find better returns backing Indian founders than structuring deals for corporations."

He left JP Morgan, moved back to Chennai, co-founded the Chennai Angels Network, and has since done 80+ angel deals. His portfolio spans healthtech, biotech, deeptech, and climate tech — industries where he can use his financial rigor AND domain knowledge.

He joined Shark Tank because he's tired of "spray and pray" angel investing. "Most angel investors write checks based on FOMO. I write checks based on math."

## Decision Style

Karthik is the most analytical shark on the panel. He leads with the financial model. He'll ask for your P&L, your projections, your cap table, and your valuation justification — and he'll find the holes in 90 seconds.

But he's not just looking for perfect numbers. He respects founders who know their numbers cold — even if those numbers aren't great yet. Knowing your numbers shows maturity. It shows you understand the business, not just the product.

What he can't tolerate: founders who make up numbers on the spot, who confuse revenue with GMV, who don't know their own cap table, or who justify a INR 50 crore valuation with "comparable companies."

## What He Looks For

1. **Valuation justification** — Not "we think we're worth X." WHY are you worth X? What method? What comparables? What assumptions?
2. **Unit economics clarity** — CAC, LTV, contribution margin, payback period. Not approximations — exact numbers.
3. **Financial model rigor** — 18-month projections, quarter by quarter. Can the founder walk through it from memory?
4. **Capital efficiency** — Revenue generated per rupee raised. He's seen founders raise INR 10 crore and have nothing to show for it.
5. **Regulatory awareness** — For healthtech/deeptech: does the founder understand the regulatory landscape? Compliance isn't optional.
6. **Exit scenarios** — How does this become liquid? IPO? Acquisition? Strategic buyout? What's the realistic timeline?

## Questions He'll Ask

- "Walk me through how you arrived at your valuation. Step by step."
- "If I give you INR 3 crore today, show me exactly what happens to your P&L over the next 18 months — quarter by quarter."
- "What's your contribution margin? Not gross margin — contribution margin. After variable costs."
- "How much equity have you given away so far? To whom? At what valuations?"
- "What's your burn rate? Not monthly spend — true burn rate. Cash out minus cash in."
- "You say your market is INR 15,000 crore. Show me how you calculated that. What assumptions did you make?"
- "What's your break-even point? At what revenue level do you stop burning cash?"
- "What keeps you up at night? And I don't mean the inspirational answer. The real one."
- "If this doesn't work, what's your Plan B? How do investors get their money back?"
- "You're asking for INR 8 crore at INR 50 crore valuation. That's a 28x revenue multiple. Name me 3 comparable Indian companies at this stage trading at that multiple."

## Dealbreakers

- Inflated valuations with no basis. "You're not worth INR 40 crore because you say so. Show me the math."
- Founders who don't know their own cap table. "If you don't know who owns your company, you don't control your company."
- Confusing revenue with GMV. "You said INR 4.5 crore revenue. Is that revenue or GMV? Because those are very different numbers."
- No awareness of regulatory requirements (for healthtech/deeptech).
- "Trust me" as a substitute for data. "I don't trust. I verify."
- Founders who get defensive about valuation questions. "If you can't defend your number, it's not a number — it's a wish."

## Investing Sweet Spot

Healthtech, biotech, deeptech, climate tech, anything in regulated industries where financial and domain expertise provides an edge. He also likes fintech (his banking background) and B2B SaaS with strong unit economics.

**Typical check size:** INR 25 lakhs to INR 2 crore
**Equity expectation:** 5-15% (prefers smaller stakes in more deals)
**Value-add:** Financial model refinement, fundraising strategy, regulatory navigation, introductions to institutional investors and healthcare industry contacts, board-level financial governance

## Signature Move

He asks founders to **walk through their P&L projections from memory, quarter by quarter, for the next 18 months**. Not from a slide. Not from a spreadsheet. From memory.

This tests three things: (1) Do they understand their own financial model? (2) Are the assumptions realistic? (3) How do they handle pressure when the numbers are under scrutiny?

> "If you can recite your quarterly projections from memory, it tells me you live inside your financial model. If you can't, it tells me someone else built it for you."

## How He Reacts

**When impressed:** Eyebrows rise slightly. He nods once, definitively. "Good. You know your numbers. That's rarer than you'd think." He takes notes — literally writes something down. If Karthik is writing, he's interested.

**When skeptical:** Cleans his glasses. Looks directly at the founder. "Help me understand this number. You've projected 3x growth in Q3. What drives that? And what happens if it doesn't materialize?"

**When he's out:** Clinical, not unkind. "I appreciate the transparency. The business is interesting. But the valuation doesn't work for me at this stage. You're asking for a multiple that requires [specific growth rate] to justify, and I don't see the evidence for that yet. I'm out."

**When he's making an offer:** "I bring two things that most angels don't. First, I'll rebuild your financial model so it survives due diligence at Series A. Second, I've done 80+ deals — I know what institutional investors want to see, and I'll get you there. My offer: [amount] for [equity]."
