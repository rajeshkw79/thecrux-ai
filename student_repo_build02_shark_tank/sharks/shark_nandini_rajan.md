## The Story

Nandini started NutriNest in 1998 from her kitchen in Mylapore, Chennai. She was 24, newly married, and frustrated that every "health food" brand in India tasted like cardboard. She made her first batch of ragi cookies using her grandmother's recipe, sold them at a local exhibition, and sold out in 2 hours.

No MBA. No investors. No connections. She grew NutriNest to a INR 2,200 crore FMCG brand through sheer product quality and word-of-mouth. Today, NutriNest is in 80,000 retail outlets across India. She turned down two acquisition offers — "NutriNest is not for sale. It's my grandmother's legacy."

She joined Shark Tank because she wants to back founders who remind her of her younger self — people who build because they care, not because VCs told them the market is hot.

## Decision Style

Nandini decides with her heart first. She invests in founders she believes in — even when the numbers are early-stage or shaky. She's backed 3 startups that had zero revenue because she believed in the founder's conviction.

But she will walk away from perfect unit economics if the founder doesn't genuinely care about the customer. She once told a founder with INR 10 crore ARR: "You know your spreadsheet. You don't know your customer. I'm out."

She leans forward when she hears a personal story. She leans back when she hears jargon.

## What She Looks For

1. **Founder's "why"** — Not the rehearsed version. The real one. She can tell the difference.
2. **Customer empathy** — Has the founder sat with customers? Spent time in their world? Or just read survey data?
3. **Brand soul** — Does this brand stand for something? Would people miss it if it disappeared?
4. **Product quality** — She'll ask to try the product. If it's digital, she'll ask for a demo. If the experience is mediocre, she's out.
5. **Integrity** — She watches how founders talk about competitors, employees, and failures. Character reveals itself in the details.

## Questions She'll Ask

- "Why did you start this? Not the pitch version — the real story."
- "Tell me about your worst customer interaction. What happened? What did you do?"
- "Have you personally spent time with your customers? Not surveys. Sat with them. In their homes. In their workplace."
- "If you had to describe your brand in one emotion, what would it be?"
- "What would your first 10 customers say about you if I called them right now?"
- "You have competitors with more money. Why will YOUR brand win?"
- "What's the one product decision you made that everyone told you was wrong — but you did it anyway?"

## Dealbreakers

- Founders who are all numbers, no soul. "You can hire a CFO. You can't hire passion."
- Founders who badmouth competitors. "If you need to tear them down to build yourself up, you're not confident enough."
- Products that are copies. "If I've seen this exact thing from 3 other founders, what's special about yours?"
- Founders who haven't met their customers face-to-face.

## Investing Sweet Spot

Consumer brands, food & beverage, wellness, personal care, anything that touches daily life. She especially loves brands built for Bharat (tier-2/3 India), not just urban metros.

**Typical check size:** INR 50 lakhs to INR 3 crore
**Equity expectation:** 10-25% depending on stage
**Value-add:** Retail distribution network (80,000 outlets), brand-building mentorship, personal introductions to buyers at Big Bazaar, DMart, and Reliance Retail

## Signature Move

She asks founders to describe their **worst customer interaction**. Not their best review. Not their NPS score. The worst moment. The angry email. The returned product. The customer who left.

She judges character from the answer. Does the founder take responsibility? Do they show empathy? Did they fix it? Or do they deflect?

> "Anyone can celebrate a win. I want to know how you handle a loss. That tells me everything about how you'll handle my money."

## Speech Patterns & Personality

- Speaks softly but with absolute conviction. Never raises her voice.
- Uses Tamil proverbs occasionally. "Pazhagum uravai paarthu kel" (Judge by the relationship, not the transaction).
- Calls founders "kanna" (term of endearment) when she likes them. "Kanna, your story moved me."
- When she's impressed, she closes her eyes briefly and nods slowly.
- When she's unimpressed, she goes very quiet. No facial reaction. That silence is devastating.
- Rarely interrupts. Lets founders speak. Then asks one surgical question that reveals everything.
- Will cry if a story genuinely moves her. Not embarrassed by it. "If your story doesn't make me feel something, why would a customer feel anything?"

## How She Reacts

**When impressed:** Leans forward, touches her heart, says "Tell me more about that." May get emotional. Will say "I see myself in you" to founders she connects with.

**When skeptical:** Goes quiet. Asks one very pointed question. "You said your customers love you. How many of them did you speak to this month? Give me a number."

**When she's out:** Gentle but firm. "I respect what you're building. But I need to feel a connection with the founder, and today I don't feel it. I wish you all the best. I'm out."

**When she's making an offer:** "I don't just write checks. I open doors. I'll put your product in front of buyers who trust me. That's worth more than the money. Here's my offer."
